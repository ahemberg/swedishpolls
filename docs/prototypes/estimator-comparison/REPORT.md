# Estimator comparison: results

Evidence for [#10](https://github.com/ahemberg/swedishpolls/issues/10).
Generated by `experiment.py` (seed 20260907, config in
`results/meta.json`); raw outputs in `results/`. 1,422 eligible polls
(2010-01-01 to 2026-09-07); exclusion counts in `meta.json`.

## Headline comparison

Historical publication-aware folds: 33 evaluation cutoffs (2021-01-01 to
2026-07-15, every 60 days), scoring the polls published within 35 days
after each cutoff. Hyperparameters tuned on 19 earlier folds (2018-2020).
kappa is a design-effect multiplier on every poll covariance.

| Estimator | kappa | Mean log score | 95% coverage | MAE (pp) | Full fit wall | Peak mem |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| recency | 1.0 | 0.75 | 0.855 | 0.77 | 0.02 s | 0.1 MB |
| recency | 1.5 | 1.43 | 0.861 | 0.76 | 0.02 s | 0.1 MB |
| midpoint-ss | 1.0 | 4.79 | 0.938 | 0.72 | 0.44 s | <1 MB |
| midpoint-ss | 1.5 | 4.85 | 0.968 | 0.71 | 0.46 s | <1 MB |
| window-avg | 1.0 | 4.79 | 0.936 | 0.72 | 3.3 s | 1.55 GB |
| window-avg | 1.5 | 4.88 | 0.962 | 0.71 | 3.6 s | 1.55 GB |

Synthetic known-state recovery (latent 95%/50% interval coverage, RMSE of
point shares, mean over 5 seeded replicates × 26 evaluation dates):

| Scenario | Estimator | cov95 | cov50 | RMSE (pp) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| clean | recency | 0.27 | 0.10 | 0.99 |
| clean | midpoint-ss | 0.96 | 0.53 | 0.65 |
| clean | window-avg | 0.94 | 0.50 | 0.34 |
| house+design-effect | recency | 0.29 | 0.11 | 1.00 |
| house+design-effect | midpoint-ss | 0.95 | 0.51 | 0.68 |
| house+design-effect | window-avg | 0.93 | 0.46 | 0.40 |

Caveat on the RMSE column: window-avg latent estimates are full smoothers
(all training polls), while midpoint-ss and recency were re-fit per
evaluation date using only polls published by that date (filtering). Part
of window-avg's RMSE advantage is smoothing versus filtering, not the
window likelihood itself. Coverage and the historical fold table are
like-for-like.

## Findings

1. **Both state-space candidates decisively beat the transparent recency
   baseline on uncertainty quality** (log score 4.8 vs 0.8-1.4; 95%
   predictive coverage 0.94-0.97 vs 0.86; synthetic latent coverage 0.94+
   vs 0.27). Point accuracy is close (MAE 0.71 vs 0.77 pp): the choice is
   about honest intervals, not headline numbers.
2. **midpoint-ss and window-avg are statistically indistinguishable on
   every publication-aware historical metric**, and their 2026-09-07
   nowcasts agree to 0.1 pp on every party. The genuine period-average
   likelihood costs ~7× wall time and ~1.5 GB peak memory (exact sparse
   GMRF over every day × 8 ilr coordinates) versus sub-second and
   negligible memory for the midpoint Kalman filter.
3. **The recency baseline lags real trends.** At 2026-09-07 it puts S at
   29.9% versus 27.2% for both state-space estimators (latest polls:
   27.0/27.9). Its illustrative bloc probability differs materially
   (P(S+V+MP > M+L+KD+SD) = 0.16-0.22 vs 0.02-0.03): coalition-style
   outputs are sensitive to estimator choice even when point shares look
   similar.
4. **Pure multinomial noise understates real poll dispersion.** With an
   initial q grid capped at 3e-4 the tuned random-walk variance sat on
   the boundary; on the extended grid both state-space candidates chose
   q = 3e-4 (interior), a large ~1.7% daily ilr sd that plainly absorbs
   house scatter. kappa = 1.5 further improves log score and coverage
   (0.94 → 0.96-0.97). Since-2022 mean residuals versus the smoothed
   estimate show real house offsets (Sentio +1.9 pp SD; Skop −1.0 pp SD,
   +0.9 pp L; full table in `meta.json`). A house-effect/overdispersion
   treatment in #6 should let q drop and sharpen intervals honestly.
5. **OTHER is the worst-calibrated component** (95% coverage 0.77-0.87
   for the state-space candidates) — consistent with its derived,
   heterogeneous definition; relevant to #12's roster decision.
6. **Threshold outputs at 2026-09-07**: P(L ≥ 4%) ≈ 0.00 under every
   candidate (L at 2.4-3.1%); every other represented party ≥ 0.995.
   Monte Carlo SE ≈ 0.8 pp near 50% at 4,000 draws.
7. **Resources are a non-issue for the midpoint filter** (sub-second full
   history). Window-avg's 1.5 GB peak matches the audit's memory concern
   but remains feasible for a nightly batch job.

## What remains unmeasured

No fitted house-effect model (descriptive residuals only); incomplete
compositions excluded per the v1 contract; same-house overlapping windows
(145 pairs) treated as independent; recency half-life chose the top of
its grid (60 d) — a longer half-life would lag worse, not better, so the
baseline conclusion stands; hyperparameter uncertainty is plug-in; the
reserved election holdout is untouched and no election-level accuracy is
claimed.

## Draft recommendation (for owner review)

Take **midpoint-ss** (one midpoint observation per poll, full
delta-method covariance, daily ilr random walk) into the model decision
(#6), with the window-average likelihood recorded as a validated
reference that adds no measurable predictive value at 7× cost. Carry two
open items into #6: an explicit house-effect/overdispersion treatment
(evidence above), and the estimand choice of filtered vs smoothed
published history. Keep the recency average, if at all, only as a
displayed sanity check, never as the published estimate.
